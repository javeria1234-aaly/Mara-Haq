## Legal Analysis: Women's Right of Inheritance in Pakistan

## 1. Executive Summary

The right is vested, not granted: Under Islamic law as applied in Pakistan, inheritance passes to every legal heir at the instant of death. Mutations, gifts and family settlements can only record or (if validly proved) transfer; they do not create the right.

The law is strong on paper; enforcement is the failure: Constitution, Shariat Act, MFLO, PPC ss. 498A–C, three provincial/federal Ombudsperson statutes and a consistent line of Supreme Court and Federal Shariat Court decisions all protect women's shares. The recurring problem is deprivation through male-dominated revenue records, "oral gifts", and informal relinquishment.

The evidentiary burden has shifted toward the party defeating the woman's share: Recent Supreme Court decisions (2026) require strict proof of every ingredient of an oral gift and refuse to treat mutations or long possession as proof.

Relinquishment by women is treated with deep suspicion: It has been held void as against public policy and Sharia (Ghulam Ali).

Multiple forums exist: Civil court (title and possession), revenue authorities (mutation, partition), Ombudsperson (summary possession relief), criminal court (s. 498A) and, for constitutional issues, High Court/Supreme Court/Federal Shariat Court.

Key risks for the project: unverified citations in source material, shifting validity and scope of the Ombudsperson Acts, and procedural details that vary by province.

## Islamic sources

- Quran 4:11, 4:12, 4:176 fix the shares of children, spouses, parents and siblings. Daughters, wives and mothers are fixed-share heirs ("ashab al-furud").

- Courts have repeatedly described inheritance as a divine right that cannot be defeated directly or indirectly (Syeda Fauzia Shah v. Federation of Pakistan, PLD 2025 FSC 1, Abrar Hussain v. Mst. Bibi Shahida, PLD 2026 SC 42).

## 4. Doctorines

Vesting of inheritance: Inheritance opens on death. Each heir becomes co-owner in the

Proportion fixed by shariah. Following are the consequences:

- A sister's share exists even if no mutation was ever recorded in her name.

- Silence or non-claim for years does not by itself extinguish it. The Supreme Court (Noor Muhammad v. Ghulam Haider, 2026) rejected limitation where the right had vested on death and was defeated only by an unproven gift.

Adverse possession by a co-heir is not readily accepted against Islamic-law inheritance

## Nature of mutation (intiqal)

Mutation is a fiscal and record-keeping entry, not a source of title. Pakistani courts have

long held this case, Noor Muhammad confirms that mutation entries and subsequent

revenue proceedings do not by themselves prove a gift. Practical effect: an erroneous or

fraudulent mutation is challengeable in a civil suit for declaration and cancellation even

after many years.

## Gift (hiba) under Muslim law

A valid gift requires:

- Declaration/offer by the donor,

- Acceptance by the donee,

- Delivery of possession (actual or constructive) to the donee.

Registration is not the test of validity for a Muslim gift but a written deed is often relied

on. In inheritance disputes the burden to prove all three ingredients lies on the donee,

through independent and reliable evidence. A gift by a father/mother of the entire land

to sons, excluding daughters, is scrutinised closely, particularly if the donor retained

possession, the donee never took possession, or the gift came shortly before death.

## Relinquishment (tanazul / dastbardari)

- Relinquishment of a share not yet reduced into possession is analytically different from a transfer for consideration. Courts have held that relinquishment by female heirs in favour of brothers, in the context of social pressure, is contrary to public policy and Sharia (Ghulam Ali v. Mst. Ghulam Sarwar Naqvi [PLD 1990 SC 1]).

- A woman who genuinely sells her share for full, proven consideration to a stranger or relative stands in a different position from one who "surrenders" it for nothing. The law protects free, informed, compensated transactions.

## 6. Civil Litigation Strategy and Trial Stages

## Framing the plaint


- Plead heirship and share first, then the specific fraud or defect (no possession delivered, forged thumb impression, no independent advice, no payment, mutation sanctioned without notice to her).

- Seek declaration plus consequential relief (cancellation, possession, partition, injunction). A bare declaration risks a later objection under Specific Relief Act s. 42 proviso.

- Join all subsequent purchasers; a mutation chain often ends in an innocent-looking third- party sale. The doctrine of bona fide purchaser may be raised, but a purchaser from a donee with a defective title generally acquires no better title.

## Interim relief

- Order XXXIX CPC injunction against alienation should be sought at the start because the practical danger is rapid sale after the defendant is served.

- Ask for an order that the revenue record be marked with pending litigation.

## Evidence plan & Responses for Defence

- For Heirship: FRC, death certificate, CNICs, succession certificate

- Absence of gift: Witnesses to the deceased's lifestyle and possession; revenue crop records (girdawari) showing who cultivated; rent/produce receipts

- Defective mutation: Certified mutation file; absence of notice; witness testimony of patwari/tehsildar

- Oral gift by father: Strict proof required; demand evidence of offer, acceptance, delivery; show donor kept possession

- She relinquished voluntarily: Void as against public policy; no independent advice; no consideration; burden on defendants

- Long possession / adverse possession: Possession of co-heir presumed on behalf of all; adverse possession not readily accepted against Islamic inheritance

- Third-party bona fide purchaser: Chain traced to a void transaction; check notice and consideration

## 7. Case Law Analysis

## Ghulam Ali v. Mst. Ghulam Sarwar Naqvi (Supreme Court, around 1990–91)

- Facts (as summarised): relinquishment of inheritance by a female heir was relied on by male relatives.

- Holding: relinquishment was void and ineffective; inheritance had accrued on the father's death and remained intact; no adverse possession in Islamic law.

- Importance: the foundation authority against informal waivers.


## Syeda Fauzia Shah v. Federation of Pakistan*, PLD 2025 FSC 1

- Federal Shariat Court emphasised that inheritance is a divine right which cannot be curtailed directly or indirectly, and that procedural formalities cannot conceal injustice. A press analysis refers to the petitioner as "Syeda Fouzia Jalal", so confirm the correct title from the law report.

## Abrar Hussain v. Mst. Bibi Shahida*, PLD 2026 SC 42 (heard 29 Aug 2025; Athar

## Minallah and Irfan Saadat Khan JJ.)

- Appeal by a man claiming property through gift documents; dismissed with costs. The Court relied on the FSC precedent and treated depriving women of inheritance as contrary to public policy and a social evil.

## 8. Reform Proposals

- Mandatory notice to all female heirs before any inheritance mutation is sanctioned.

- Digital alert and biometric confirmation by each heir at mutation.

- Statutory presumption of invalidity for relinquishments/gifts by women to male relatives unless supported by independent legal advice, a registered deed and proof of consideration.

- Fast-track tribunals with strict timelines (as proposed in Punjab Bill 63 of 2025).

- Legal aid desks at Arazi Record Centers and NADRA SFUs.

- Single, consistent national Ombudsperson framework with clear constitutional footing.

## 9. Practical Roadmap for a Woman Claiming Inheritance

- 1. Document: FRC, death certificate, CNICs, existing mutations (request certified copies), jamabandi/fard.

- 2. Check the record: is her name entered with the correct share?

- 3. Freeze: bank notice of death; excise caveat; objection at the revenue office.

- 4. Trace: FBR, SBP, CDC, MTMIS (README Section 5).

- 5. Seek legal advice from an advocate or legal-aid body before signing anything.

- 6. Choose forum: Ombudsperson for fast possession relief (after verifying current validity), civil suit for title, criminal complaint in serious fraud.

- 7. Never sign a relinquishment, gift deed or power of attorney without an independent lawyer, understanding of its effect and receipt of actual value.

- 8. Preserve evidence: messages, witnesses, photographs of possession, receipts.
